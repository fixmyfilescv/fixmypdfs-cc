Compress a PDF to 500 KB

500 KB is generous enough that most documents reach it with nothing noticeable lost. A twenty-page scan usually fits with the text layer intact, and a text report fits many times over. This is the target to choose when you have a say in it.

By Suprabhat · Updated September 21, 2026 · How we write these

Who asks for 500 KB?

  • Email systems and CRMs with a per-attachment cap.
  • Content management and intranet systems limiting uploaded documents.
  • Tender and procurement portals accepting supporting documents.
  • Anyone attaching several documents to one message under a shared limit.

What to expect at 500 KB

A 20-page text report
Far under the limit already. Compression is probably unnecessary.
A 20-page scan
Comfortable, keeping the text layer and readable quality throughout.
A photo brochure
Achievable. Photographs will be softer on close inspection but fine on screen.
A 100+ page scan
Tight. Consider splitting, or compressing to a larger target if the limit allows.

How to do it

  1. 1 Confirm the file actually needs it Text-only documents are often well under 500 KB already. Compressing one that fits gains nothing.
  2. 2 Set the target to 500 KB Type it, or pick the nearest preset. The work happens in the browser, so a large scan is limited only by your device.
  3. 3 Run with flattening off At this size it is almost never needed, and leaving it off guarantees the document stays selectable and searchable.
  4. 4 Spot-check two pages The densest page and the one with the most graphics. The before-and-after view scores what changed on each.
  5. 5 Send it Same pages, same order, bookmarks and links intact — it is an ordinary PDF that happens to be smaller.

When compressing is the wrong answer

Compression works on images, which is why it does very little to a document that is mostly text. A 900 KB text report is 900 KB because of its fonts and structure, not because of waste, and squeezing it returns an 870 KB report.

When a text-heavy document is genuinely too large, the size is usually coming from something specific: embedded full-resolution logos, a scanned page pasted into an otherwise digital document, or attached files riding inside the PDF. Finding that one page is more effective than compressing all of them.

The other honest answer is to send less. Extracting the pages the recipient asked for beats compressing ninety pages they did not.

Questions

How much can I expect a scanned PDF to shrink?

Scans typically drop by 70 to 90 per cent, because they are photographs stored at more resolution than anyone needs. Text-based PDFs often shrink by under 10 per cent — there is little to remove.

Will bookmarks and links survive compression?

Yes. Compression rewrites the images inside the file; the outline, links, page order and text layer are carried through unchanged.

Should I compress twice to get it smaller?

No. A second lossy pass approximates an approximation and the damage accumulates visibly around text edges. Always start again from the original with a smaller target.

Is 500 KB small enough to email?

Comfortably — every mail provider allows many times that. 500 KB limits usually come from a system rather than from email, such as a CRM or a document management tool.
